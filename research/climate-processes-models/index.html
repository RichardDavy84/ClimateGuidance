Research / Climate Processes & Models

Why does the same energy change produce different warming?

Boundary-layer physics, temperature variability and careful model evaluation connect mechanisms to the climate patterns we observe.

Start with the volume of air

Heat distributed through a shallow layer of air can produce a larger temperature change than the same energy distributed through a deeper layer. With Igor Esau, I examined how this physical perspective helps explain differences in climate-forcing efficacy.

Days, nights and variability

The lower atmosphere changes over the day and with weather conditions. Work with Igor Esau, Alexander Chernokulsky, Stephen Outten and Sergej Zilitinkevich examined how this contributes to asymmetric warming. Related work connects boundary-layer representation with modelled temperature trends and variability.

Evaluate the process as well as the average

A model can reproduce one large-scale measure while retaining important regional or seasonal biases. My work on CMIP and Arctic surface climate asks which processes support: or undermine: the interpretation of those outputs.

One thread from Mars to Earth

My doctoral work on the Martian atmosphere and NASA’s Phoenix mission began with questions about the lowest part of an atmosphere. The environments differ enormously, but the discipline of connecting observations, physical processes and models has remained central to my work.
