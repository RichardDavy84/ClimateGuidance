Before we start

Frequently asked questions

What climate models and datasets do you use?

I acquire and process the datasets appropriate to the question and region. Options include CMIP global climate-model ensembles, CORDEX regional simulations, ERA5 reanalysis and seasonal forecasts. Processing can include extracting variables, quality checks, spatial and temporal aggregation, and deriving indicators for analysis.

How does downscaling help with local decisions?

Downscaling can represent local gradients associated with terrain and other surface properties. KrigR, developed with Erik Kusch, provides one route to statistically downscaled reanalysis. Each application includes an assessment of the information the inputs can support.

What is convection-permitting modelling?

These simulations use fine grids to represent deep convection more directly. They can be useful for questions about intense rainfall and complex terrain, with the choice of dataset guided by the region and available evaluation.

Why work with a scientist when climate data are public?

Finding the right data is the starting point. I acquire, quality-check and process the relevant observations and model output into a form suited to your analysis, explain uncertainty and keep the calculation traceable.

What can a project deliver?

A project can produce spatial data, tailored indicators, a scientific briefing, a report, an interactive dashboard or a workshop. We agree the scope, methods, handover format and any ongoing updates at the outset.

Can you build a tool around our planning timescale?

Yes. We start with the decision, location, time horizon and operational thresholds. That defines which observations, forecasts or projections are useful and how an interface should present them.

Can you provide training and support?

Workshops can cover choosing datasets, interpreting maps and ensembles, understanding uncertainty and using a commissioned tool. Documentation, training and maintenance can be included in the project scope.
